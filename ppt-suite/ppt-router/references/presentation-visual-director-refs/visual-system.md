# Visual System

Use this reference to establish a coherent visual contract before slide implementation. User-provided templates, brand standards, and explicit art direction override these defaults.

## 1. Communication hierarchy

Give each slide one primary message and one dominant visual element. The dominant element may be a statement, number, image, or chart. Supporting elements must be visibly subordinate.

Prefer editorial composition over interface composition. Avoid defaulting to card grids, pills, badges, tabs, and dense dashboard structures.

## 2. Typography

Use the following ranges as starting points for 16:9 Chinese or bilingual business decks. Select a consistent point within each range for the deck; do not oscillate page by page.

| Role | Default range |
|---|---:|
| Cover title | 40–48 pt |
| Cover subtitle | 20–26 pt |
| Slide title | 28–34 pt |
| Key statement or number | 32–52 pt |
| Section or module title | 20–24 pt |
| Body | 17–21 pt |
| Caption | 13–16 pt |
| Footnote or source | 9–12 pt |

For spacious keynote-style decks, use the upper half of each range and less copy. For information-dense briefing decks, use the lower half while preserving legibility.

Rules:

- Shorten or split content before reducing body type below the selected contract.
- Keep one-line slide titles on one line.
- Avoid more than three meaningful type sizes on one content slide, excluding sources.
- Use approximately 1.15–1.35× line height for Chinese body copy, adjusted for the font.
- Avoid exaggerated letter spacing in Chinese; generally keep it at or below 2% of font size.
- Keep line length comfortable. A full-width paragraph should rarely span the entire canvas.

## 3. Grid and spacing

- Use consistent left and right safe margins, normally 5–7% of slide width.
- Align titles, body blocks, images, and charts to a small number of shared anchors.
- Use spacing steps rather than arbitrary gaps. A practical visual rhythm is 8/16/24/32/48 in the implementation's coordinate system or a proportional equivalent.
- Preserve breathing room around the dominant element.
- Avoid both crowded edges and empty areas without compositional purpose.

## 4. Image roles

Assign every meaningful image exactly one role before placement:

| Role | Typical share of slide | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| Hero | 45–70% | Main visual or emotional anchor |
| Split image | 40–60% | Balanced image-text composition |
| Background | 100% | Atmosphere, with a safe text zone or scrim |
| Evidence | 30–55% | Product, document, place, or proof requiring inspection |
| Supporting | 20–35% | Secondary explanation |
| Accent | under 15% | Non-essential decoration only |

Treat these as hierarchy targets rather than mechanical quotas.

Rules:

- Do not use a meaningful image as a postage-stamp thumbnail. Promote it, move it to another slide, or remove it.
- Usually use one primary image per slide. Multiple images require a comparison, sequence, collection, or other explicit relationship.
- Read the source aspect ratio before choosing the container.
- Use complete display when edge content, diagrams, screenshots, packaging, or evidence must remain visible.
- Use a focal-safe crop for photography only when the subject and required context remain intact.
- Match or generate the image for its intended frame; do not force a portrait subject into a shallow landscape crop.
- Place the subject away from the planned text zone when sourcing or generating images.

## 5. Preferred archetypes

Use a restrained set appropriate to the narrative:

- minimal cover;
- chapter divider;
- one statement plus one strong image;
- 45/55 or 55/45 image-text split;
- key number plus supporting evidence;
- chart plus one explicit takeaway;
- two-way comparison;
- timeline or process when sequence is essential;
- closing statement or call to action.

Vary adjacent silhouettes while retaining shared anchors, typography, color, and spacing.

## 6. Density tiers

- **Breathing:** one statement or image, minimal support.
- **Standard:** one core message with two to four supporting points.
- **Briefing:** a chart, table, or structured evidence with a concise takeaway.

Do not turn every slide into the briefing tier. Add a breathing or transition slide when the narrative needs pacing.

## 7. Visual contract example

```text
Style: editorial minimal, calm and credible
Density: standard with occasional breathing pages
Typography: 32 pt titles, 19 pt body, 12 pt sources
Grid: 6% side margins; shared left title anchor
Images: one primary image; 45–60% on image-led pages; focal-safe crops
Archetypes: statement+image, 55/45 split, chart+takeaway, chapter divider
Avoid: small decorative images, card grids, pills, heavy gradients, repeated silhouettes
Review: inline renders; final PPTX only after approval
```
